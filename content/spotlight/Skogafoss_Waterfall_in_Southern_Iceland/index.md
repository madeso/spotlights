Skógafoss waterfall flows just south of the Mid-Atlantic Ridge, known locally as the Reykjanes Ridge, a mountain system that's very slowly tearing Iceland apart. Even in a country brimming with plenty of spectacular waterfalls, the mighty torrent of Skógafoss makes a dramatic impression. It's one of the biggest cascades in the country, with a width of roughly 24 metres and a drop of 60 metres.

Monumental sights like Skógafoss naturally spawn legends – it's said that the area's first Viking settler hid a treasure chest behind the waterfall. The story goes that when the chest was eventually discovered, it disappeared when someone attempted to grab it. Of course, the real treasure of Skógafoss is its breathtaking beauty, which a great many photographers have captured in all seasons and from nearly all angles.
