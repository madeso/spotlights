These bright blue and yellow fish gather together above coral reefs in either shoals or schools. What's the difference? A shoal is a group of fish that, for social reasons, stay together in a relatively loose formation. A school is a shoal that has transformed into a coordinated group that swims in a tight, synchronized pattern, usually for protection from predators or for more efficient feeding. Yellowback fusiliers are certainly beautiful, no matter how they hang out, but when they mass in the hundreds as the sun filters through seawater to sparkle on their scales, they make a grand display. The species is found throughout the Indo-Pacific—this bunch of beauties was photographed off the coast of the Maldives in the Indian Ocean.
