'Museum City' is a moniker often used to describe Khiva, an ancient city in northwest Uzbekistan, and our view of its old town walls is a sublime example of why the name fits. The foundations of these mudbrick fortifications are said to date back to the 10th century, with crenelated walls rising nearly 33 feet built in the late 1600s. Inside the walls of this part of Khiva, known as Itchan Kala, are more than 50 historic monuments and hundreds of well-preserved old homes. In 1990, Itchan Kala became a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and in 1997, Khiva as a whole celebrated the 2,500th anniversary of its founding.
