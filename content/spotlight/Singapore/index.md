This may be one of the world's smallest countries, but it certainly doesn't lack in things to see. Besides extensive urban gardens, Singapore boasts forests of ultramodern skyscrapers. Historically under the control of myriad kingdoms and empires—from the Siamese to the Portuguese to the Dutch to the British—Singapore was part of Malaysia until trade disagreements led to its expulsion in 1965. Newly independent, the island port quickly rebounded as perhaps the last true city-state, relying on its strategic location and a policy of virtually tariff-free trade. Now the busiest seaport outside China, Singapore is one of the world's most diverse and cosmopolitan cities: Around 40% of its residents are foreign-born.
