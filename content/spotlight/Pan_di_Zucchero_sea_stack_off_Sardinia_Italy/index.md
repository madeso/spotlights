Some accounts say that Pan di Zucchero (Sugarloaf), a massive limestone sea stack, was named because of its resemblance to Brazil's Sugarloaf Mountain. Or it could be simply because from some angles it looks a bit like a gargantuan sugar cube floating in the bright blue Mediterranean Sea. However it got its name, it's a stunning sight from the shore of Masua Beach, where our image was captured. This area on Sardinia's southwest coast was once a centre of a booming mining industry, and not far this impressive islet is another extraordinary sight hewn from rock: along the coastline is a port and harbour carved directly into the cliff face. Porto Flavio was built in 1924 to aid delivery of iron ore to waiting steamships moored below the sheer cliff. While no longer in use, it remains an impressive sight.
