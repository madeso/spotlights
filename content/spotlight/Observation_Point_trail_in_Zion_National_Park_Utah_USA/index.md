Zion National Park is one of the most visited national parks in the United States, but when the area in southwestern Utah became a national monument in 1909, it was extremely remote and virtually inaccessible to visitors. Even after it became a national park in 1919, fewer than 4,000 visitors came to Zion the following year. But in 1930, the Zion-Mt Carmel Highway was completed, linking Zion to the Grand Canyon and Bryce Canyon, and greater numbers became acquainted with this geological marvel.

Zion is defined by its breathtakingly steep red cliffs, its maze of sandstone canyons, and its waterfalls with colourful hanging gardens. Visitors here can trek for miles, ducking into crevices and resting in canyons once occupied by the Ancestral Puebloans. Our image shows part of the Observation Point trail, a marvellous route to one of the most spectacular viewpoints in the park.
