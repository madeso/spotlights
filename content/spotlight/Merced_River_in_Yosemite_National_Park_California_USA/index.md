More than 120 miles of the Merced River are officially protected as a Wild and Scenic River. And for much of its total 145-mile course, the Merced River's 'wild and scenic' description is entirely on point.

The California river makes its way through canyons, mountains and foothills, flowing from the Sierra Nevada through the southern part of Yosemite National Park – seen here in our image. It then tumbles over Nevada Fall and Vernal Fall before calming significantly in the Yosemite Valley. The Merced River eventually flows out of the park and into the San Joaquin Valley. On some stretches, rafters are tested by Class III and IV rapids, and visitors can camp at several sites along the river's course while also exploring the Merced River Trail.
