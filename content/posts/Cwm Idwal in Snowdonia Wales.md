A cirque, or 'cwm' in the Welsh language, is a kind of semicircular valley formed by glacial erosion. Our image is a sweeping view across Cwm Idwal and the Glyderau mountains that tower above it, within Snowdonia National Park. In addition to its undeniable scenic value, this area is said to be the most southerly portion of Great Britain where Arctic plants can grow. Elevations are high enough here that visitors may well see bright-pink blooming clusters of moss campion, or delicate tufted saxifrage poking their petals through cracks in cliffs. And speaking of rugged terrain: Cwm Idwal is a popular place for rock climbing.
