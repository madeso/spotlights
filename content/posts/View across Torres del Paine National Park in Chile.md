The Cordillera Paine, a small but stunningly dramatic mountain range, is the centrepiece of southern Chile’s Torres del Paine National Park. We see a portion of the range in our image, called the Paine Horns, named after those sharp and flinty peaks. And while the mountains here are undeniably breathtaking, they’re only part of an array of landscapes in the 700-square-mile wilderness. Wide green valleys, impossibly blue lakes, and hulking glaciers fill out the rest of the park, which happens to be much closer to Antarctica than it is to Chile’s capital city of Santiago, nearly 1,900 miles to the north.

Torres del Paine isn’t an easy place to get to, and it’s this very remoteness that adds to the near-mythic nature of the park. Indeed, this region of Patagonia is nicknamed ‘the end of the world’.
