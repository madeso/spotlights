Legend tells that these tall rock formations were to be the pillars for a pier built by a monk, but that a demon thwarted those plans, so the deck was never finished. In our image, we see just a few of the many hulking sea stacks, collectively known as Hashigui-iwa, that march in a straight line into the sea. This fantastic landscape is part of Japan's Yoshino-Kumano National Park, a large noncontiguous nature reserve that ranges over three prefectures, including the home of these rocks, the coastal Wakayama Prefecture.

The parklands not only cover a lot of oceanfront, but also a great deal of inland mountain terrain, including a namesake of the park—Mount Yoshino. The mountain has its own camera-fodder to rival these coastal rock formations: Some 30,000 cherry trees that burst into bloom each spring.
