Ngorongoro Crater – said to be the world's largest intact and inactive volcanic caldera – isn't just a monumental geological feature in northern Tanzania. This 600-metre-deep crater and its 100-square mile valley floor are home to a huge array of wildlife. In our image we see a sampling of the abundant animals that roam the plains, including those mingling in the forefront: wildebeest and zebras. The pink smudges gathered on the alkaline lake in the background are a large flock of flamingoes, primarily lesser flamingoes.

The nature reserve located within the crater is the Ngorongoro Conservation Area and it protects the animals in our image, as well as many others such as black rhinos, hippos, African buffalo and a small population of lions. Almost every species of wildlife in East Africa is thought to be represented within this breathtaking natural wonder.
