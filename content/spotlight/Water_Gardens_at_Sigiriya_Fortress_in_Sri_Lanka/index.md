Sigiriya (aka Lion's Rock), a fortress built atop a dramatic rock formation nearly 180 metres tall, is considered one of the best examples of ancient urban planning still intact. King Kashyapa ruled Sri Lanka until the late 5th century, and he developed Sigiriya as not only a defensive bastion but an elaborate self-contained settlement. Our image features a view of Sigiriya rock from its base, where landscape gardens were laid out as part of the fort complex. Ingenious engineering went into these water features: The fountains, constructed with underground conduits some 1500 years ago, still work today.

Following the king's death, the site became a Buddhist monastery that stood until the 14th century. Today the ruins and beautiful gardens of Sigiriya are a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
