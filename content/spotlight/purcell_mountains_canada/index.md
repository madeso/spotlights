Here below Mount Monica, one of the peaks in the Purcell range, larch trees serve as a seasonal marker: Although they are conifers, larches are not evergreen, and unlike their relatives, the larch's needles turn a golden yellow in autumn—as shown in our photo—before falling to the ground when winter approaches. In every season, the landscape of the Purcell Mountains is awe-inspiring, and because this region in southeast British Columbia is more remote than some of the other scenic hotspots farther west, you may get it all (at least mostly) to yourself.
