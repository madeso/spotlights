The volcanic island of Hachijō-jima lies within Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park, a large nature reserve that extends across the Izu archipelago. Located in the Philippine Sea roughly 120 miles south of Honshu, Japan's largest island, Hachijō-jima has a humid subtropical climate and gets a lot of rain—on average, 120 inches annually. This climate supports the island's lush terrain and varied wildlife. The most renowned plants here are undoubtedly the island's bioluminescent mushrooms, notably the Mycena lux-coeli, or 'heavenly light mushrooms.' While looking for the mushroom's pale green light, fungus seekers may also encounter pygmy date palms, hibiscus, and bird of paradise plants.

Somewhat bigger treasures are to be glimpsed beyond these shores, as humpback whales have made the waters around Hachijō-jima their breeding grounds since 2015.
