Tide pool on Tenerife, Canary Islands
One of the world's most popular holiday destinations is, essentially, set on the top of a volcano. Tenerife last saw an eruption of Mount Teide in 1909, but the volcano is still very much active. This largest island in Spain's Canary Islands chain shows evidence of a fiery birth throughout its terrain – ancient lava tubes, fields peppered with huge boulders, deep ravines and beaches strewn with sea stacks abound on Tenerife.

We can see one of those sea stacks in the background of our image, which offers a hint of the moody magnificence of the island's coast. Along with rugged black-sand beaches punctuated by tide pools, the nearly 42-mile stretch of Tenerife shoreline also features areas of soft white sand and truly outstanding surf breaks.
