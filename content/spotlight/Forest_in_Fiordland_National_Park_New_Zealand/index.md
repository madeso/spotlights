On the southwest coast of New Zealand's South Island is the jagged, glacier-carved landscape of Fiordland National Park. And while many photos of the place are incredible bird's-eye view panoramas, our image captures the verdant scenery found deep within one of the park's dense forests. This coastal region is the southernmost portion of the Southern Alps, and Fiordland National Park is the largest of New Zealand's national parks, with an astounding area of 4,868 square miles. It's also a significant part of the wider Te Wahipounamu World Heritage Site. The Indigenous Māori people lived in this area for centuries before the Europeans arrived.
