From this high vantage point (you could say 'balloon-level' view) we're looking across an expanse of the vast Namib-Naukluft National Park. How vast? Well, let's just say it's only a glimpse of the more than 19,000 square miles of the extraordinarily beautiful Namib Desert landscape in western Namibia. Within the park, some of the world's highest sand dunes rise in an area called the Sossusvlei, with the dark range of the Naukluft Mountains their only competition on the horizon. The Namib is thought to be the oldest desert in the world, and perhaps nowhere in the park feels more ancient than the Deadvlei, a place where long-dead skeletal trees still stand planted in the bright orange sands. But Namib-Naukluft National Park is far from lifeless: animals thrive here, including a kind of antelope exquisitely adapted to its challenging environment – the gemsbok.
