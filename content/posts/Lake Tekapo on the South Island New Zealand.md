Here on New Zealand's rugged South Island, the waters of Lake Tekapo display such striking electric-blue colours because of glacial silt carried into the lake from the flows of the Godley and Macauley Rivers. These rivers are themselves fed by the melted waters of glaciers in the nearby Southern Alps. As those active glaciers slowly bulldoze the mountainsides, they pulverise rocks along valley floors. All that grinding eventually produces a fine-grained powder of silt, sometimes called glacial flour. Meltwater picks up the silt, carrying it downstream and into lakes like Tekapo. The particles are so light and fine, they're slow to sink to the bottom, remaining suspended in the water column instead.The aquamarine jewel of Lake Tekapo is set within a wider region of incredible landscapes known as the Mackenzie Country, a vast, sparsely populated basin within the Southern Alps.
