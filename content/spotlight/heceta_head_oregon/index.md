This headland on Oregon's central coast is named for explorer Bruno de Heceta, who led an expedition in 1775 to reinforce Spain's claim to the Pacific Coast of North America. After sailing from Mexico, the crew was ravaged by scurvy by the time they reached these shores, and Heceta made the decision to turn back—but not before becoming the first to map and provide a written description of the mouth of the Columbia River, as well as this rocky, 1,000-foot-high headland that would later bear his name. Two centuries later, construction of a lighthouse began—complicated by steep bluffs and the remote location—and the first beam of light pierced the darkness in 1894.
