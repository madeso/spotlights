The Mekong Delta is known as the 'rice bowl of Vietnam', and with good reason: the majority of the nation's rice crop is grown in this southwestern region where the Mekong River empties into the South China Sea. Our aerial image shows patchwork paddies, dotted with stands of trees, in the Ta Pa fields of An Giang province, one of the delta's top rice-producing areas. The relative flatness of the delta makes the terrain ideal for cultivation – in fact, about a quarter of Vietnam's entire agricultural output comes from the Mekong Delta.

Besides fertile farmland, the delta's roughly 15,000 square miles contains a great deal of biodiversity and is home to over a thousand animal species. It's also a place of great cultural riches: here in An Giang province, evidence of the ancient Óc Eo culture has been uncovered. And the area is known for its silk production as much as its prodigious rice output.
