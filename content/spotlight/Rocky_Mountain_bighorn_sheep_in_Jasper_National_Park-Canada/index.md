The name of the animals in our image is about as straightforward as you can get: bighorn sheep. And the subspecies we're looking at is the biggest bighorn of all. Basking in a bit of sunshine in Jasper National Park, a vast wilderness reserve in Alberta, are two Rocky Mountain bighorn sheep. Males, or rams, commonly weigh over 300 pounds, with 30 pounds carried in their impressive, curving horns alone.

Do these rams ram? Absolutely—each autumn, male Rocky Mountain bighorn sheep will joust for dominance by facing each other, rearing up on back legs, and pitching forward to butt heads, reaching speeds of up to 40 miles per hour. And while just witnessing that display may give us humans a headache, bighorn sheep have skulls designed to absorb most of the impact, so the dramatic clashes aren't particularly dangerous.
