Angkor Wat, the sprawling temple complex in northwest Cambodia, is indisputably a location of great historic and cultural significance—but predating those ruins are the remains of Vat Phou in southern Laos, a site that was a thriving religious hub some 700 years before the first stones of Angkor were set. Considered a sacred spot since ancient times, with its first brick structure built in the 7th century CE, Vat Phou is one of Southeast Asia's oldest places of worship. Most of the ruins here offer glimpses into a storied past, but one sanctuary shrine contains Buddha statues and is still an active place of worship
