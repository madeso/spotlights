While officially called the Guangxi Guilin Huixian Karst Wetlands, many people who've seen this extraordinary landscape refer to it as the 'mirror' or 'glass' fields. When water fills the rice paddies and marshes, and the sunlight hits them just right, this lush locale indeed glimmers like glass. The Huixian wetlands are a Ramsar Site, a designation that bestows certain protections on areas deemed ecologically important and in need of careful conservation. There's precious cultural history to preserve here, too: the remnants of Gui Liu Canal, constructed in roughly 700 CE to control irrigation and prevent flooding, still stand as an example of ancient engineering.
