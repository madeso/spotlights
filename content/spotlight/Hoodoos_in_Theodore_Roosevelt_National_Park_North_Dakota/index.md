We're looking at a section of surreal rock formations—known as hoodoos—in Theodore Roosevelt National Park, located in the badlands of western North Dakota. Native Americans, early explorers, and traders each had their own name for the so-called badlands, but the point they were all trying to communicate is that this land of canyons and strange rock formations was tough to traverse. In 1883, Theodore Roosevelt traveled to these badlands and fell in love with the lifestyle and freedom he experienced. After he became president, he established 18 national monuments and six national parks including this one, the only US national park named after a person.
