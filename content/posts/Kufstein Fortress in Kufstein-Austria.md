The Inn river, a tributary of the Danube, flows below Kufstein Fortress, the most renowned landmark in the city of Kufstein in western Austria. The fortress is certainly set in a conspicuous position—atop a large hill rising from the middle of the city. The high vantage point was chosen for its strategic, not scenic, potential, and the site has seen several fierce battles through the centuries.

Dating from at least as far back as 1205 (when it was first mentioned in the historical record), Kufstein Fortress has been captured and surrendered by regional powers and royalty many times over. One of those royals was Emperor Maximilian I, who in 1522 added the Kaiserturm, the large round tower we see in our image.
