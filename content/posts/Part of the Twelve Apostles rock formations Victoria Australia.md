A staircase leads down from the high cliffs of the Gibson Steps and onto a beach where – as we can see in our image – people can get up close and personal with a few of Australia’s iconic limestone stacks called the Twelve Apostles. The high coastal cliffs and these surf-washed stacks are part of Port Campbell National Park. There were never really a dozen of these hulking sea stacks, there were nine of them at the start of the 21st century. By 2009, two of the formations succumbed to the unrelenting pounding of the Indian Ocean waves and collapsed into the surf. Despite today’s single-digit population, the name Twelve Apostles simply has too much recognition to change. However, the waves continue to dig at the cliffs above the beach, and may one day carve new stacks to join the ranks.
