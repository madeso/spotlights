The Seimon Ishibashi bridge is reflected in a moat near the Imperial Palace in the heart of Tokyo. This is the emperor of Japan's main residence, and was constructed on the site of Edo Castle, built in 1457 and home to the ruling shogun during the Edo Period. Perhaps the most extraordinary features here in the heart of the world's most populous metropolitan area are the sublime parks and gardens. Some of these peaceful havens are off limits to visitors, but others – such as the beautiful Kitanomaru Park, once part of the Edo Palace's grounds – are public parks. A bit of palace trivia: amid Japan's property boom of the 1980s, the roughly half-square-mile comprising the Tokyo Imperial Palace and its grounds was valued to be worth more than all of California's real estate combined.
