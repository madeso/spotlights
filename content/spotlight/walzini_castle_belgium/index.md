Walzin Castle looks as though it sprouted directly from the rocky ledge it sits upon. Planted atop a high promontory, a castle in one form or another has overlooked the River Lesse in southern Belgium since the 13th century. Although most of the original structure was burned down by the French in 1554, a 15th-century horseshoe tower with four cannon ports survived the blaze. Numerous restorations followed, and Walzin Castle now stands as a superb time capsule of Belgian architecture. And it doesn't stand alone—this area of Wallonia has plenty of picturesque castles, including the nearby Castle of Vêves, built around 1410.
