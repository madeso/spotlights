The 225-square miles of Los Nevados National Natural Park sit within the green heart of the Colombian Andes, protecting one of South America's extraordinary ecosystems. This is the high country, where snow-dusted volcanic peaks rise to the heavens. It's also home to something else that touches the sky – here the world's tallest palm trees stretch upwards of 60 metres tall. These Quindío wax palms are especially plentiful in the park's Cocora Valley, seen in our image.

Because these trees are relatively rare, the national park's protections are essential for their survival. Los Nevados also conserves precious wetlands that support a variety of wildlife. And the park's river-feeding glaciers supply water to millions of people as well as to some of the richest coffee-growing regions of Colombia.
