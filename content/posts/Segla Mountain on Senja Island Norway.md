Lying above the Arctic Circle is Norway's second-largest island. Senja sits off the northwest coast of the mainland, and its name likely derived from an Old Norse phrase meaning 'split apart.' The moniker may have been inspired by the myriad fjords cutting deeply into the island's coast. Above those waterways loom steep and flinty mountains. Perhaps none is more iconic than the peak in the center of our image. Segla rises more than 2,000 feet above the small village of Fjordgård, seen (quite tiny) on the lower left of our image. Segla's sheer western face fronts the Mefjorden, where fishing boats ply the waters alongside guided tour boats on the lookout for sea eagles, the occasional whale, and all that spectacular scenery.
