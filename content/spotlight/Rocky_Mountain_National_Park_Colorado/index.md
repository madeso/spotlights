One of the most distinctive features of Rocky Mountain National Park in Colorado is the variety of ecosystems it hosts. Thanks to the varying elevations of the subranges within the park, a visitor can experience forests and grasslands at lower levels, alpine woods at the midrange and tundra conditions just before the snowline. Even travelling across the Continental Divide in the park means a shift from dry, open lands on the east to dense, wet forests on the Western Slope. And speaking of that divide – some rivers in this park flow east and others west, depending on which side of the line they fall.
