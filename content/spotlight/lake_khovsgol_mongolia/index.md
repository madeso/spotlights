Nearly twice the size of Texas yet with under an eighth of its population, Mongolia is the world's most sparsely populated country. Its 604,000 square miles of steppe hold just 3.5 million people, about half in Ulaanbaatar. Here in Khövsgöl Province near the Russian border, the land rises into the Sayan Mountains, where clear, cold lakes fill long tectonic basins tucked between peaks and thick larch forests. Lake Khövsgöl, Mongolia's largest freshwater lake by volume and second-largest by area, stretches 85 miles and holds nearly 70% of the country's fresh water. Often called the Mother Sea, it's an ancient rift lake carved into the southern edge of the Siberian taiga.
