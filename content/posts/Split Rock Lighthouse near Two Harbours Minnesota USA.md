On Lake Superior's north shore stands what many consider one of the most picturesque lighthouses in the United States. The setting can hardly be beat: Split Rock Lighthouse is set atop sheer cliffs rising more than 40 metres above the lakeshore in Minnesota, and it's surrounded by birch, spruce and fir trees. This beacon was built as an essential aid to mariners after a terrible storm on the lake took many lives. In 1905 the Maatafa Storm struck Lake Superior, damaging or destroying some 29 vessels and killing 36 people. In response Split Rock Lighthouse was built, using cranes to haul materials up from the lakeshore to the top of the cliff.

First lit in 1910, the lighthouse was retired from service in 1969, but every November 10th its beam shines once again, in honour of a Lake Superior ship that was lost in another storm – the SS Edmund Fitzgerald.
