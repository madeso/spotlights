On the road between Queenstown and Arrowtown, one of New Zealand's most iconic bridges crosses the Shotover River. It's not the country's longest span (that would be the Rakaia Bridge), but it is one of the most beloved—and most photographed. The setting has a lot to do with it: A narrow gorge of forested cliffs leads down to a turquoise river, a fine tableau of Central Otago scenery. The bridge's namesake is important, too. Edith Cavell Bridge is named after a World War I British nurse who saved many Allied soldiers' lives by helping them escape from German-occupied Belgium. Around 1919, when construction was finished, a gold miner living near the bridge christened it with the heroic nurse's name, and after a while it stuck.
