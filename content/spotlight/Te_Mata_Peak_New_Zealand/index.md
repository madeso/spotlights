Cottony clouds blanket the slopes of Te Mata Peak in the Hawke's Bay region of New Zealand. The mountain rises sharply from the flat Heretaunga Plains, and those who trek (or cycle) to the summit are rewarded with sweeping views of the Pacific Ocean once the morning mist lifts. The name Te Mata is Māori in origin, from Te Mata-o-Rongokako, or 'the face of Rongokako'. According to legend, Rongokako was a giant who attempted to bite through the hills to win a lover's challenge, shaping the land in the process. Today, the peak is admired both for its natural beauty, and for the stories and cultural meaning embedded in its rugged form.
