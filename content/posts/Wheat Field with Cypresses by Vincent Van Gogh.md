A Provençal cypress tree rises over a Saint-Rémy wheat field in one of a series of landscapes Vincent Van Gogh painted in the area during 1889. The Dutch artist felt that this was among his best summer landscapes, and part of his inspiration may have come from how close he was to his subject: The canvas was painted 'en plein air', the French term for 'outdoors'. Not long after painting the scene in oils, Van Gogh created additional versions of the same view, one drawn in reed pen. Wheat fields were a favourite subject of the artist, and he created dozens of works depicting them – an entire series of his work is known as, simply, 'Wheat Fields.'
'Wheat Field with Cypresses' is in the collection of the Metropolitan Museum of Art in New York City.
