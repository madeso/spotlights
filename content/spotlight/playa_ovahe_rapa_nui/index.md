The shoreline pictured here is not the most recognizable aspect of Rapa Nui (aka Easter Island)—that would undoubtedly be its collection of massive stone statues called moai. Most visitors to this exceedingly far-flung Polynesian island 2,300 miles off the coast of mainland Chile make a beeline to the centuries-old volcanic tuff visages. But those who explore the further reaches of Rapa Nui—such as Playa Ovahe—find another kind of monumental landscape, that of eroded volcanic cliffs, pink sand beaches, and ancient caves. Ovahe is relatively isolated and a bit off the beaten path, so it's a secluded spot on one of the most remote inhabited islands on Earth.
