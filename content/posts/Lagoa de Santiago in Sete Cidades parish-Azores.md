Huge tectonic activity, occurring over a vast stretch of time, formed the Azores, an island chain in the North Atlantic Ocean. The archipelago is located above an area where three tectonic plates meet, a site known to geologists as a triple junction. In the parish of Sete Cidades on São Miguel Island, evidence of the origins of the Azores are on dramatic, and superbly scenic, display. Our image shows a portion of the Sete Cidades Massif, an enormous volcanic complex of lava cones, domes, craters, and calderas. Lagoa de Santiago, a caldera lake, lies in the center of our image, while in the background we catch a glimpse of the even larger Lagoa das Sete Cidades, a set of two lakes brimming with legend.
