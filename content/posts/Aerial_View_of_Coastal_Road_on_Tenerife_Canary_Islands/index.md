While all of the islands in the Canary Islands archipelago are gems, the one we're peering down at is the largest and best known of the chain. Tenerife's terrain is a stark, primaeval mix of deep canyons, ancient mountains, lava tubes and towering coastal cliffs. While pine forests grow in the higher elevations, much of the island reflects its volcanic origins. In the past 20 million years, Tenerife has been shaped by many volcanic eruptions, and it's thought that the island is the world's third-largest volcano when measured from its base on the sea floor.

Tenerife is visited by millions of people a year, likely due to a combination of its unusual beauty, its blissfully temperate weather and how relatively easy it is to get to: The Canary Islands are an autonomous community of Spain, and Tenerife is only 200 or so miles off the North African coast of Morocco.
