In part due to their dark plumage, until the 16th century cormorants were commonly referred to as 'sea ravens.' But they aren't closely related to ravens, and if we were being really picky, we'd point out that cormorants are also more accurately called shorebirds rather than seabirds. What is clear is the cormorant's impressive fishing and diving skills.

These are great cormorants in our image ('great' is the species name, but we think they're pretty awesome, too). They're likely resting up before diving into the Nile River at Aswan, Egypt, in search of food. Great cormorants are prodigious eaters and will sometimes nab really big fish. A large catch is no problem for these birds though—they can swallow their dinner whole. Apropos then, that the official name for a group of cormorants is a 'gulp.'
