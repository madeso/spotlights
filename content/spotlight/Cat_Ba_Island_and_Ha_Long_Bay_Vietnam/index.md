Cát Bà is the biggest of the more than 300 islands in the Cát Bà archipelago of northern Vietnam. The only major settlement on the island is Cát Bà Town, so most of this incredibly biodiverse haven is still undeveloped. A third of the island, and a portion of the bay we see beyond, are preserved as Cát Bà National Park. With little more than 110 square miles of land, Cát Bà Island contains a variety of natural environments, from mangrove swamps to rocky karst cliffs.

These cliffs are home to the island’s rarely seen, and very endangered, golden-headed langurs—sometimes called Cát Bà langurs. It's thought that fewer than 70 of these primates, a sub-species of the white-headed langur, live on the island. It's a perilously low number, but at least it's grown since 2003’s estimate of just 40. They remain one of the world’s most endangered primates
