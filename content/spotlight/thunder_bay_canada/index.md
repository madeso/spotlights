Thunder Bay is as famous for its natural beauty as it is for international shipping. As Canada's westernmost terminus of Great Lakes navigation, the city of Thunder Bay and its harbor have served as a trading and transportation hub since the 17th century. Northwestern Ontario's biggest urban center is still surrounded by thousands of square miles of wilderness, like this shoreline scene on Lake Superior. Given Thunder Bay's proximity to the famously stormy lake, it's no surprise that the city frequently experiences some seriously cold weather—in 2013 a new low record was set with a -59 degrees Fahrenheit windchill temperature.
